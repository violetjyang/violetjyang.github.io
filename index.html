About

I am a first-year Clinical Psychology PhD student at Southern Methodist University. I study how people make meaning of their impulsive behavior in acute psychopathology, using behavioral interviews and ecological momentary assessment. My research also draws on complex systems theory to understand the process dynamics that lead from impulsivity to the extreme decisions and actions that cause harm. I am also broadly interested in how young people relate to AI, the roles they assign to it, and how those relationships may shape mental health and suicide risk.

Prior to SMU, I earned a dual major in Psychology and Criminology from UC Irvine, followed by an MA in Clinical Psychology from Columbia University. As part of the Accelerating Medicines Partnership® Schizophrenia (AMP® SCZ), I served as a Clinical Research Coordinator at the Icahn School of Medicine at Mount Sinai in New York, working on the Psychosis Risk Outcomes Network (ProNET) and the Psychosis Risk Outcomes Compound Assessment Network (ProCAN), where I followed individuals at clinical high risk for psychosis longitudinally. In that role, I studied suicidality, auditory hallucinations, and self-stigma in psychosis using quantitative and computational methods, including formal theory and natural language processing (NLP).

Outside of research, I'm a freediver and an advocate for marine biodiversity under UN SDG 14 (Life Below Water).
